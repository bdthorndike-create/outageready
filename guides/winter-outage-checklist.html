Winter power outage checklist

Updated November 1, 2026 · 4 min read

Carbon monoxide first. Never run a generator, grill, camp stove or charcoal burner indoors, in a garage, or near windows and vents. Install battery-powered CO alarms and test them now. If anyone feels dizzy, nauseous or has a headache while a fuel burner is running, get everyone outside and call emergency services.

Before the storm

  • Charge phones, power banks and any battery backup.
  • Fill water containers; you can't count on pumps if you have a well.
  • Check flashlights and headlamps; keep spare batteries.
  • Know where your main breaker and any gas shutoff are.
  • Write down medication needs and keep a few days' supply if you can.

Heat

  • Close off rooms and live in one warm space.
  • Dress in layers, hat and blankets. Sleeping bags rated for cold work well.
  • Only use heaters designed for indoor use, and follow the manual.
  • Electric heat drains batteries quickly; do not plan to run it off a portable power station for long.

Food

  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed. A closed freezer holds temperature far longer than an open one.
  • Keep non-perishables that need no cooking.
  • When in doubt about food that was above safe temperature for hours, throw it out.

Power and communication

  • Use a battery for phones, router and lights first; they cost very little energy. Our calculator shows how little.
  • Keep a battery or crank radio for emergency broadcasts.
  • Save your utility's outage line in your phone.

Protect your pipes

  • If the house is going to get very cold, let faucets drip and open cabinet doors under sinks.
  • Know how to shut off your main water valve.
