How long will a power station run a CPAP?

Updated November 1, 2026 · 4 min read

Disclosure: we earn a commission on qualifying purchases made through links on this site. Details.

A CPAP is one of the easier things to back up. Many draw well under 100 W, so even a small battery can cover a night. The catch is that the answer depends heavily on your pressure setting and on whether the humidifier and heated hose are on.

Find your machine's real draw

  1. Check the power rating on the machine's label or in the manual. Manufacturers often publish average consumption figures for the machine alone and with humidification.
  2. For the most accurate figure, plug it into a cheap plug-in watt meter for a night and read the watt-hours used.

The quick estimate

Watt-hours needed = average watts × hours of sleep. Add roughly 15-20% for inverter losses. For example, a machine averaging 40 W for 8 hours uses 320 Wh, so about 375 Wh with losses.

Average draw8-hour nightWith ~15% losses
30 W (no humidifier)240 Wh~275 Wh
60 W (humidifier on)480 Wh~550 Wh
90 W (humidifier + heated hose)720 Wh~830 Wh

Those are illustrative. Your machine and settings decide the real numbers.

Ways to stretch the battery

  • Turn the heated humidifier off during an outage, or use it on a low setting. It is usually the biggest draw. Ask your provider whether that is fine for you.
  • Use the machine's DC cord if it has one. Running DC directly avoids the loss from converting to AC and back, and some units are efficient this way. Use only the cord your manufacturer approves.
  • Multiple nights? Plan how you will recharge: solar input, a car charger, or a generator that is run outdoors.

Check these before you buy

  • An AC output that works well with sensitive electronics; most reputable power stations do, but check the spec.
  • Whether your machine's manual allows use with a battery or inverter.
  • Cold performance. A battery stored in a freezing room may deliver less than its rating.
Medical equipment note: This is general information, not medical advice. If you depend on oxygen concentrators, ventilators or other life-sustaining equipment, a hobby-sized power station is not a substitute for a proper plan. Talk to your equipment provider and doctor, and ask your electric utility whether it keeps a medical-priority customer list.
